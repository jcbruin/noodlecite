Chicago Style Documentation System Overview

The Chicago Manual of Style (17th & 18th Editions) offers two distinct citation tracks:

  • Notes & Bibliography (NB): Standard in history, literature, theology, art, and the humanities. Uses numbered footnotes or endnotes in text, and an alphabetical Bibliography at the end.
  • Author-Date (AD): Common in the physical, natural, and social sciences. Uses brief parenthetical text citations (Author Year, Page) and an alphabetical Reference List at the end.

1. Books

Bibliography: Chernow, Ron. Alexander Hamilton. New York: Penguin Books, 2004.
Footnote (Full): 1. Ron Chernow, Alexander Hamilton (New York: Penguin Books, 2004), 52.
Short Note: Chernow, Alexander Hamilton, 52.
Author-Date: (Chernow 2004, 52) / Chernow, Ron. 2004. Alexander Hamilton. New York: Penguin Books.

2. Journal Articles (with DOI)

Bibliography: Davis, Natalie Zemon. "The Rites of Violence: Religious Riot in Sixteenth-Century France." Past & Present, no. 59 (1973): 51–91. https://doi.org/10.1093/past/59.1.51.
Footnote (Full): 1. Natalie Zemon Davis, "The Rites of Violence: Religious Riot in Sixteenth-Century France," Past & Present, no. 59 (1973): 55, https://doi.org/10.1093/past/59.1.51.
Short Note: Davis, "The Rites of Violence," 55.

3. Chapter in an Edited Collection

Bibliography: Morgan, Edmund S. "The Puritan Ethic and the American Revolution." In Essays on the American Revolution, edited by Stephen G. Kurtz and James H. Hutson, 3–43. Chapel Hill: University of North Carolina Press, 1973.
Footnote (Full): 1. Edmund S. Morgan, "The Puritan Ethic and the American Revolution," in Essays on the American Revolution, ed. Stephen G. Kurtz and James H. Hutson (Chapel Hill: University of North Carolina Press, 1973), 15.
Short Note: Morgan, "The Puritan Ethic," 15.

4. Websites & Online Documents

Bibliography: Library of Congress. "The American Revolution: A Selected Resource Guide." Library of Congress Digital Collections. Accessed October 1, 2026. https://www.loc.gov/rr/program/bib/revwar/.
Footnote (Full): 1. Library of Congress, "The American Revolution: A Selected Resource Guide," Library of Congress Digital Collections, accessed October 1, 2026, https://www.loc.gov/rr/program/bib/revwar/.

5. Multiple Authors in Chicago

• Two authors: Bibliography: Last1, First1, and First2 Last2. | Note: First1 Last1 and First2 Last2.
• Three authors: Bibliography: Last1, First1, First2 Last2, and First3 Last3. | Note: First1 Last1, First2 Last2, and First3 Last3.
• Four or more: In notes, give the first author followed by et al. In bibliography, give the first author followed by et al. (or up to 10 authors listed).
